# First Light: Notes With Memory and a Butler That Knocks

Lucent begins from three quietly unfashionable convictions: that what you write is nobody's business but yours, that an assistant ought to be able to do things rather than merely hold opinions about them, and that software this practical has no excuse for being ugly. What follows is a notes and tasks app with a memory, a lock, and a butler who knocks before entering — shipped in pocket and desk sizes from one shared heart.

**Notes and tasks keep their history.** Every meaningful edit is snapshotted, so you can read what a note said last Tuesday, and restore it when a rewrite proves optimistic. Type `[[Shopping list]]` and it becomes a tappable link; point at a title that does not exist yet and the link glows red until a tap conjures it. Markdown renders when you want it and stays exactly as typed when you do not. Checklists are proper citizens: reword items in place, open a roomy pop-out editor, lose nothing to a forgotten plus. Tags, colours, pinning, attachments. Tasks carry subtasks, priorities, repeat schedules and reminders that survive a reboot, and due dates are parsed from ordinary language — *next Friday at 6* becomes a genuine timestamp with a genuine alarm.

**The assistant has hands, not merely opinions.** Bring your own model or run none at all. Lucent's assistant can create, read, edit, colour, pin, archive and delete notes and tasks; before it changes anything, it says exactly what it intends and waits for your reply. Someone with an opinion and no hands is a chat. This is a butler who knocks, and it works to your instruction rather than to a schedule of its own. The tools it reaches for are the ordinary ones — a title, a tag, a colour, a due date — so a request in plain language ends as a change you can see, read back and undo.

**Everything is sealed, and rather lovely.** The database is encrypted at rest, attachments are encrypted individually, and the app lock enforces a real brute-force policy rather than issuing a polite request. Backups travel as a single portable file, previewed in full before a single item changes on the way back in, so a restore is a decision rather than a leap. Behind the glass, soft colour drifts behind frosted panels; a generous palette of themes and palettes does the decorating; four languages switch without ceremony, every label and date included. The calm is not applied afterwards as decoration; it comes from the same decisions as the encryption.

One product, two sizes: a signed Android APK and a Windows installer, built end to end by pressing a button on GitHub and going off to put the kettle on. Business logic, data and most of the interface live once, and each platform adds only the seams that genuinely belong to it. Notes with memory, tasks with teeth, an assistant with manners, encryption that keeps its promises.
