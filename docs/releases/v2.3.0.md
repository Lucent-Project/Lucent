# A Spring Clean, Undertaken in September

This release is a set of standards rather than a set of novelties: sixteen items, four new Monet themes and 126 background colours, applied to the Android build and the Windows installer in the same pass. Lucent runs on one heart across two machines, and a clean is the moment that design pays its dividend — a hundred small surfaces brought to one standard on one afternoon. There is no wrong month for it.

**The private area is a place you visit.** A note placed in the hidden area opens its detail page on demand, and the hidden list shows it as a rounded-square card in a grid, beside the hidden tasks that share the same home. The query layer admits notes and tasks on identical terms, so the private area behaves like a room with a door: you go in, you read, you come out. Nothing stands between a hidden note and the reading of it. The history pages are fully token-coloured throughout, so a restored version sits in the same palette as everything around it.

**Lists return their rows cleanly.** Removing an item and undoing the removal is a two-gesture pleasure, and the animation is part of it. The lists settle their rows back politely after a delete, so a bottom sheet that confirms its own disappearance does so without appearing to break up with the screen. Restoring an old note version records the state it replaced as a fresh history entry, timestamped at that moment, whether the restore comes from the interface or from the assistant's own tools. The scroll-edge control is one disc in one place, and its arrow turns to face the direction it will take you.

**The overdue section celebrates completion.** A task finished is a task finished, and the task world agrees with the run world: ticking an overdue item clears it from the overdue section on the spot, sub-requests included. Chat holds to the same standard. "Ask again" sits small and bottom-right, outside your bubble; the conversation header carries no redundant conversation button; attachments appear as filename rows, each one opening in the viewer with a tap and no ceremony. Settings keeps small-model optimisation in its own card under Memory, and the model dropdown searches fuzzily by name, so a half-remembered identifier is enough.

**Both platforms were cleaned together.** The checkbox, the scroll position and the toast mean the same thing on Android and on Windows. The lock screen runs a brute-force ladder with attempt counts, adaptive error tints and an optional self-destruct, and the lockout copy speaks four languages. Crash Shield installs itself at launch when enabled. A backup taken reports its own success or failure, and doodle exports merge a note's ticked canvases into one multi-page PDF. Four new Monet themes join the palette, and the 126 background colours contain no duplicate. The background drift keeps its own 30 fps governor, so page changes cost it no frames.

The result is the feeling of a good tidy: you cannot point to one object, but the room is bigger. The private area opens, the lists settle, the overdue section forgives, the dialogs hold their corners, and both machines keep the one-heart rule. It is a philosophy, not a schedule.
