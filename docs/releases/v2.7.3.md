# Dates Follow the Language, and the Details Follow Suit

A week of living with a release is a stricter reviewer than any checklist, and this is the pass that week produces: dates that change language with the interface, a fold with room to breathe, template chips that match their neighbours by construction, a background that negotiates its own frame budget, and a first set of shared-logic tests standing guard over the palette's arithmetic. Nothing here is large. Everything here is exact.

**Dates that follow the language you choose.** Switch Lucent to Chinese and walk back to the list, and the date under every note and task is Chinese — on the same frame, with no restart and nothing to wait for. One shared helper in Glass.kt keys the formatted caption on the timestamp and the active language together, so every composed card re-renders the moment the language changes. Android and Windows draw on the same shared source and give the same answer. A date is a piece of grammar, and it now belongs to the language it is written in, on both machines.

**A fold with a frame, and chips of one cloth.** The "More options" row now sits a clear 16 dp above the attachment row in every composer, on both platforms: a door with a frame around it. Custom template chips wear FilterChipDefaults, so they share the built-ins' rounded rectangle, fill, outline and label style, and the strip reads as one family at a glance — only authorship tells a custom chip apart. Both changes are small and exact, the kind you notice every time you open a composer and never have to think about. Spacing, it turns out, is a form of grammar too.

**A background that negotiates its budget.** A governor watches the real gap between delivered redraws. Six consecutive late ones drop the cadence a tier — 30 to 24 to 20 frames per second — and eighteen on-time redraws climb it back. On these slow drifting curves 20 fps is visually indistinguishable from 30, and the frame budget it frees is exactly what a page transition on a weaker GPU needs. When it steps down it also sheds the most expensive part of the dark-mode pipeline. A device that holds 30 fps sees none of this: the governor acts only on evidence.

**Tests that hold the promises to their numbers.** The first shared-logic unit tests now run on every build: template and saved-search round-trips, the password-attempt ladder, and two tests that pin the palette — exactly eighteen tints, exactly thirty-six boards, every entry unique, every hidden entry still resolving a stored key. On Windows the suite runs before any native work begins, so a rule that breaks is reported in minutes, while the engines are still quiet. The figures in this release are therefore not marketing; they are assertions the build will defend. One suite now answers for both platforms.

Switch the language and the dates follow. The fold has its frame, the chips are one family, and the background asks only for the frame time it can afford. Android and Windows keep step from the same shared sources, and the small exact steps are the ones now covered by tests.
